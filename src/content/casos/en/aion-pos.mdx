## Context

Collaborative Riwi project (2026): a POS system for restaurants and coffee shops, built as a working MVP.

## Problem

A restaurant or coffee shop needs to manage its menu, orders, bookings, customers and administration. The goal was to do all of it from a single platform.

## My responsibility

I was the team's **Product Owner** under Scrum: I defined, prioritized and tracked features. I also took part in development, especially:

- Bookings.
- Order management.
- Users.
- Multi-tenant structure.

## Analysis

Scope was defined from business needs and split into modules. Tasks were written as user stories and prioritized by sprint, focusing on what a working MVP needed.

## Solution

System modules:

- Authentication and roles.
- Staff and administration.
- Customers.
- Bookings through QR code or link.
- Menu.
- Orders and delivery.
- Stock.
- Invoicing.
- AI-assisted recommendations.

## Implementation

- **Multi-tenant:** every record is tied to its business, keeping each restaurant's data separate.
- **AI recommendations:** use the menu and the preferences or context of the order to suggest dishes from what each business offers.

## Validation

The system's flows were tested, especially authentication, bookings, orders and data separation between businesses. The team also reviewed the work during sprints.

## Result

A working MVP with the POS's main modules.

There is no production metric for improvement or adoption, so no figure is reported.

## Learnings

I learned to combine a technical perspective with defining and prioritizing features as Product Owner, and strengthened my understanding of multi-tenant architecture in applications built around real business processes.

Today I would define acceptance criteria and success metrics from the start, because they make validating the product much easier.
